# Historical Adventure Game — User Manual

Welcome to the world of historical exploration! This learning system invites you to explore like an adventurer, discovering stories from the past through conversations with historical figures. Here, there are no standard answers, only infinite possibilities for exploration.

---

## What is This System?

**Historical Adventure Game** is a mission-based historical learning platform that allows you to:

- **Converse with Historical Figures** — Talk with people from different eras, listen to their stories and experiences
- **Explore Like a Detective** — Ask questions, collect clues, discover the truth behind historical events
- **Understand Multiple Perspectives** — Different people have different viewpoints on the same historical event
- **Deepen Your Thinking** — Reflect on the feelings of historical figures, understand why they acted as they did

This is not a course about memorizing historical timelines. Instead, you will be like a historian, gradually building your understanding of history through questioning and listening.

---

## Game Flow Quick Guide

This system is divided into **6 stages**. Let's learn about them step by step:

### **Stage 0: Choose a Mission**

Before beginning, you need to select a historical mission.

**What You'll See:**

- Mission cards showing the era, location, and brief introduction
- Difficulty hints (helping you choose an appropriate challenge)

**What You Can Do:**

- Click on a mission card to read more details
- Select the historical scenario that interests you most

  **Tip**: There's no "correct" choice between "easy" or "difficult". Choose a mission that interests you, and your learning will be most effective!

---

### **Stage 1: Decode the Background Story**

Now you enter that era. The system will present a partially hidden historical background.

**What You'll See:**

- Main information about the story background
- Some parts marked with "□□□" (like redacted files)
- Mission objectives (telling you what to explore)

**Why Are Some Parts Hidden?**

This simulates reality: our understanding of history is often incomplete. Your task is to gradually fill in these gaps through conversations with characters.

**What You Can Do:**

- Carefully read the visible information
- Think: "What do I want to know?"

---

### **Stage 2: Choose Your Conversation Partner**

Now you need to select the first character to interview.

**What You'll See:**

- 3-4 character cards
- Each card displays: name, occupation, historical era, and a brief self-introduction

**Example Characters:**

- **Xiao Qing** — A student at a public school, representing the perspective of ordinary people
- **Sato Keiichi** — A police officer, representing the official/law enforcement perspective
- **Yamamoto Kansuke** — A land surveyor, representing the technical official's perspective

**Why Choose?**

Different people have different understandings of the same situation. A student might focus on school policies, police might emphasize legal aspects, and a surveyor might discuss technical and land issues.

**What You Can Do:**

- Choose any character to start the conversation
- You can converse with other characters later

  **Tip**: There's no "correct" order of choice. But we recommend conversing with all characters in sequence for the most complete understanding.

---

### **Stage 3: Deep Interview & Exploration**

This is the core learning stage! You will converse with the selected character.

**What You'll See:**

| Interface Section     | Description                          |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Left — Chat Box**    | Your conversation record with the character |
| **Right — Notebook**   | Important clues and keywords collected by the system |
| **Bottom — Input Box** | Where you can enter your questions    |
| **Suggested Questions** | Three types of prompting questions from different angles |

#### **How to Ask Questions?**

You can ask any question. There are no wrong questions.

**You Can Ask:**

- Basic facts: "What is your job?"
- Personal experiences: "What did you experience that day?"
- Motivation and feelings: "Why did you do this?" "Were you afraid then?"
- Historical context: "How did this affect people's lives?"
- Follow-ups and deeper inquiry: "You just said... could you tell me more?"

#### **Three Types of Suggested Questions**

The system may provide three types of question suggestions:

1. **📍 Factual Questions**

   - Asking "what is"
   - Helps you understand basic information about historical events
   - Example: "What was the specific process of that investigation?"

2. **⚡ Conflicts and Contradictions**

   - Asking "why different people have different views"
   - Helps you understand the complexity of historical events
   - Example: "What impact did this policy have on local people?"

3. **💭 Empathy and Feelings**
   - Asking "about the character's feelings and motivation"
   - Helps you think from another's perspective
   - Example: "How did this experience affect you?"

#### **Learn While Conversing**

- **Clues are automatically collected**: When a character mentions important information, the system automatically adds it to the "Notebook" on the right
- **No time pressure**: Talk as long as you want, ask 50 questions if you like

  **Secrets to Effective Conversation:**

- **Listen carefully** — Character responses often contain multiple layers of information
- **Follow up** — "Can you tell me more?" is often the best question
- **Notice contradictions** — If a character's answer differs from what you heard before, that's normal and interesting!
- **Follow your curiosity** — Don't just ask the suggested questions. Ask what you want to know

---

### **Stage 4: File Repair — Matching Clues**

Now, the system presents a historical file waiting for repair, with some blank spaces.

**What You'll See:**

- Right: All clues and keywords you collected in the conversation
- Left: Blank spaces in the historical file

**Your Task:**

- **Drag** each clue to its corresponding blank space
- Complete the entire file

**How Does This Help?**

- Verify understanding: You need to judge which historical fact each clue belongs to
- Internalize knowledge: Hands-on manipulation helps with memory
- Build connections: See the complete links, understand the full picture of events

💡 **Tips**:

- You don't have to get it right the first time. You can try multiple times
- If unsure, try it! You can correct mistakes
- Pay attention to hint text, they will help you judge

---

### **Stage 5: Completion Confirmation**

Congratulations! You've completed this mission.

**What You'll See:**

- The complete historical file (all blanks have been filled)
- A completion seal or certificate
- A brief summary of the historical scenario you explored

**What You Can Do:**

- Read the complete historical record
- Review your learning achievements
- Choose to undertake another mission

## How to Learn Effectively

Learning history is not for tests or scores, but to **understand people**. Here are some suggestions to help you gain maximum learning effectiveness from this system:

### **1. Converse with Multiple Characters**

Different people have different understandings of the same event.

- A student might focus on education
- Police might emphasize order and law enforcement
- A surveyor might discuss technology and land

**What Can You Learn?**

- History is not a single story, but many perspectives woven together
- The same event can have multiple "truths"

### **2. Notice Similarities and Differences Between Characters**

When conversing with different characters, compare their answers:

- Do they have the same view on the same thing?
- If different, why? Is it because they are in different positions?
- What social reality does this reflect?

### **3. Imagination and Empathy**

The most important skill in history is **empathy**:

- Think from that person's perspective: "If I were them, what would I do?"
- Ask about their feelings and fears
- Think about why they did what they did, rather than judging right or wrong

### **4. Track Changes**

Notice changes and conflicts in what characters discuss:

- "You said X before, now you say Y, what happened?"
- "Different people have opposite views on the same thing, what does this show?"

### **5. Draw Your Own Conclusions**

The system won't tell you what to think. This is important:

- After hearing multiple perspectives, think for yourself
- "What do I think the truth is?"
- "What does this teach me?"

## Frequently Asked Questions

### **"What if I don't know what to ask?"**

That's completely fine! It's normal. Try these methods:

1. **Use suggested questions** — The system gives you three suggested questions each time, you can click to use them directly
2. **Imitate a news reporter** — Ask "Who? What? When? Where? Why?"
3. **Follow your curiosity** — Did something the character said interest you? Follow up on it
4. **Imagine you're a detective** — What information do you need to piece together the complete story?

### **"Can I ask the same question twice?"**

Yes, but the character's answer might be different or more in-depth. If you want to understand more details, follow-up questions are a great learning method.

### **"Is there a time limit?"**

No. Talk as long as you want.

### **"Do I have to converse with all characters?"**

Not necessarily, but we **strongly recommend**:

- Converse with at least 2 characters to compare their perspectives
- Converse with all 3 characters for the most complete understanding

Different characters have different information and different views. Hearing multiple voices deepens your understanding of history.

### **"What if my answer is 'wrong'?"**

This system has no wrong answers; different perspectives are all valid.

- Characters answer based on their knowledge and experience
- Sometimes they don't know certain things either
- Sometimes characters contradict each other, which reflects history's complexity

### **"What if the character doesn't answer my question?"**

Possible reasons:

1. **Beyond their knowledge domain** — They may not have experienced or heard of this
2. **Involves sensitive topics** — Some topics characters may be unwilling to discuss (this is also part of history)

Try asking from a different angle, or ask the character about other things they know.

### **"Can I have multiple conversations with the same character?"**

Yes! In fact, we recommend you do. Each conversation might spark new thinking.

## 🌍 Things to Remember

### **1. Characters Have Their Own Perspectives**

Everyone you meet here is telling **their version of the story**:

- They have their own experiences
- They have their own positions
- They may not know all the facts

Your job is not to judge them right or wrong, but to **understand why they see it that way**.

### **2. No One Knows Everything**

In history, everyone's knowledge is incomplete.

- Common people don't know official decisions
- Officials may not know people's feelings
- Everyone only sees the world within their own perspective

This is why **multi-angle conversations are important**.

### **3. Different Answers Can Both Be "True"**

"He says A, she says B, who's telling the truth?"

The answer is: **Both could be true**, because they each saw different aspects. History is not black and white.

### **4. History is the Story of People**

Finally, remember this:

> History is not about memorizing dates and event names.  
> History is about **understanding people—their choices, feelings, struggles, and hopes**.

When you understand the characters' motivations and feelings, you understand history.
